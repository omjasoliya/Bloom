Privacy

Your files stay on your computer.

Online converters need a copy of your file. That puts private documents, photos and their hidden details on a server you don't control. Bloom does the work with tools that run on your machine: ffmpeg for video and audio, LibreOffice for documents, and built-in PDF and image libraries.

Uploading adds risk

Your file leaves your computer, so you have to trust how another service stores, processes and deletes it.

Bloom works locally

Conversions, tools and actions run on your own computer. It works offline, behind a firewall, and on files you'd never upload. The only thing it ever asks the internet is whether a new version exists, and only if you allow it.

    Download anyway

    Windows 10 and 11, 64-bit. ffmpeg comes built in. Install LibreOffice (free) to convert documents, spreadsheets and slides. On Windows 11, Open in Bloom sits under Show more options.

    Or from a terminal (picks Intel/AMD or ARM for you)
    $ wget -O /tmp/bloom.deb https://github.com/omjasoliya/Bloom/releases/latest/download/bloom_$(dpkg --print-architecture).deb
$ sudo apt install /tmp/bloom.deb

    Ubuntu 22.04+, Debian 12+, Linux Mint and Raspberry Pi OS (64-bit), on Intel/AMD or ARM64, in an X11 (Xorg) session. Not sure which you have? Run uname -m: x86_64 is Intel/AMD, aarch64 is ARM64. ffmpeg is installed with it. Install LibreOffice (free) to convert documents, spreadsheets and slides. On Wayland the key wheels can't see drags.

    Android 10 and newer. Bloom isn’t on the Play Store yet, so Android asks once to allow installs from your browser, and Play Protect may ask you to confirm. Share a file to Bloom and the result is saved in Downloads/Bloom. Bloom works offline; it only goes online to check for new versions, and only if you say yes when it asks. On phones Bloom converts photos, PDFs, videos and audio.
